## Team

Qonstruct is developed and maintained by the team at the **Institute of Architecture of Application at the University of Stuttgart**. The project brings together research, teaching, experimentation, and practical application development.

Our work is shaped by collaborations across **academia and industry**, with numerous experiments exploring how complex technologies can be made more accessible and understandable.

## History

Qonstruct started with a simple question:

**How can we make quantum computing more accessible?**

Quantum computing offers entirely new possibilities for computation, but working with quantum technologies can be challenging. New programming models, unfamiliar concepts, specialized hardware, and complex development environments can create significant barriers for people who want to explore the field.

Qonstruct was created as a low-code platform that aim to make application development and experimentation more accessible. Instead of requiring users to express every idea through code, Qonstruct provides visual concepts that allow users to build, connect, and understand computational processes.

This becomes particularly relevant in an age of AI. AI-assisted programming can make it easier to generate code, but it also introduces new questions about how we understand, control, and reason about the systems we build. We believe that even in an age of AI, there is value in **deterministic approaches, explicit structures, and understandable concepts**.

Qonstruct therefore explores an alternative perspective: rather than hiding complexity behind generated code, we can make important parts of an application **visible, structured, and understandable**. Users can work with explicit components, relationships, and processes and see how these elements contribute to the behaviour of an application.

This approach is particularly important when experimenting with emerging technologies such as quantum computing. Visual and low-code representations can provide an accessible way to explore concepts that would otherwise require a substantial amount of programming knowledge.

## QUORUM4H

The project **QUORUM4H** is an important part of this exploration. It investigates approaches for making quantum computing more accessible and connecting quantum technologies with people from different backgrounds.

Together with Qonstruct, QUORUM4H reflects our broader interest in developing **accessible, visual, and understandable ways of interacting with complex computational systems**.

Our experiments span research and teaching as well as collaborations with industry. They explore how low-code environments, visual representations, deterministic structures, and emerging technologies can work together to create new ways of developing and understanding applications.

## Our approach

Qonstruct is based on the idea that **not everyone needs to become a programmer in order to create with technology**.

Low-code does not have to mean giving up control or understanding. Instead, it can mean providing better abstractions: concepts that are simple enough to understand, explicit enough to reason about, and powerful enough to build meaningful applications.

In a world increasingly shaped by AI, we see value in combining the possibilities of generative technologies with **deterministic and transparent approaches**. AI can help us explore possibilities and accelerate development, while explicit structures and visual concepts can help us understand and control what we build.

Qonstruct is an ongoing exploration of this space between **code and no-code, AI and deterministic systems, experimentation and understanding, research and practical application**.

## Get involved

Qonstruct is developed as an environment for experimentation, research, teaching, and collaboration. We welcome students, researchers, developers, designers, and industry partners interested in exploring new approaches to application architecture, low-code development, quantum computing, and emerging technologies.

Whether through research projects, prototypes, experiments, or collaborations, Qonstruct provides a space to explore a fundamental question:

**How can we make complex technologies accessible without making them incomprehensible?**
