## Overview

A group combines related parts of a model into one unit that you can collapse and expand. Groups reduce the visual complexity of large models and let you look at individual parts of a circuit separately.

## Creating a group

1. Click the **Group selection** (lasso) button above the canvas controls in the bottom-left corner.
2. Drag a selection area around the nodes you want to group. Nodes that are only partly inside the area are included too.
3. Release the mouse. The selected nodes are combined into a group, and the lasso mode ends.

A group needs at least two nodes. To leave the lasso mode without grouping, click the button again (**Cancel grouping**).

![Lasso button in the canvas controls](../images/lasso_button.png)

## Group types

The type of a group is determined automatically from its nodes:

| Type      | Contains                              |
| --------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Quantum   | Only quantum operations               |
| Classical | Only classical operations             |
| Mixed     | Both quantum and classical operations |

Each type has its own border color and corner shape, so you can see what a group contains without expanding it.

![Group types](../images/grouping_types.png)

## Working with groups

The header of a group contains these controls:

| Control                  | Effect                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Title                    | Double-click the title to rename the group.                                                               |
| **Collapse / Expand**    | Shows the group as a single compact node, or shows its content again.                                     |
| **Lock / Unlock**        | A locked group is protected against changes to its content and its position. It has a less transparent background. You can still collapse, expand and ungroup it. |
| **Ungroup**              | Dissolves the group. Its nodes return to the surrounding model at the same relative positions, and their connections are restored. |

You can also:

- **Nest groups:** a group can be part of another group, so you can structure a model on several levels.
- **Duplicate groups:** select a group and press **Ctrl + D**, or copy and paste it, to reuse it elsewhere in the model.

## Connections

Connections between nodes inside a group and the rest of the model are routed through proxy ports on the border of the group. The content of the group stays encapsulated, and all existing connections are preserved, also while the group is collapsed.

## Groups and validation

Groups only structure the model; they don't change its meaning. The nodes inside a group are validated like any other node. In the [Math Mode](./math-mode), selecting a derivation step highlights only the affected node inside a group, not the group itself.
